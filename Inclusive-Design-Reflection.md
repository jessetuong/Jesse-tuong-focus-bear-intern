# Research & Learn

- Vulnerable populations include neurodivergent individuals (ADHD, Autism), people with mental health conditions, and people with disabilities. Their challenges include sensory overload from busy UIs, unclear/inconsistent navigation, notification overwhelm, and rigid workflows that don't match how their brain works.
- Ethical considerations for neurodivergent design: avoid overwhelming UX (clutter, excessive animations, sudden pop-ups), respect sensory needs (adjustable brightness/sound, no forced autoplay), and ensure clear, literal communication - avoid idioms/sarcasm in copy.
- Accessibility for ADHD/Autism: simple, direct language; short sentences; predictable, consistent navigation and layout; reduce cognitive load - one action per screen, minimal choices at once.
- Supporting neurodivergent teammates: be explicit and direct in communication (avoid vague asks), put things in writing rather than only verbal/async voice notes, and respect different working styles/hours - allow flexibility.

# Reflection

- Communication style: be more literal and specific, avoid assuming shared context, confirm understanding rather than assuming it.
- Common pitfalls: dense text walls, unclear CTAs, notification spam, guilt-driven copy ("You broke your streak!"), too many settings/decisions at once.
- Practical change: default to short, scannable messages and ask "does this need a follow-up or clarifying question?" before sending.

# Task

- First-person account reviewed: [Productivity Tools for ADHD and How I Get Things Done](https://witanddelight.com/2025/02/work-routine-productivity-tools-for-adhd/)
- Design improvement idea: replace streak-loss guilt messaging with neutral, encouraging re-engagement copy (e.g., "Welcome back - let's pick up where you left off" instead of "You broke your streak")
- Practice: supportive response to a hypothetical user struggling with executive functioning

**User:** "I keep opening the app and then just staring at my task list. I can't even start."

**Response:** "That sounds really frustrating - task paralysis is common, and it's not a motivation problem, it's a starting problem. Let's shrink this down: pick just one task, and instead of 'do the task,' set a 2-minute action like 'open the document.' You don't have to finish, just start. Want to try that with one item on your list right now?"
